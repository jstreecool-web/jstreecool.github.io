Executive summary

Group work, Laamanen CAS M&A module (27 Nov 2026): design an acquisition program for Legend Holdings (HKEX: 3396), with AI technology as the investment focus. This page covers design elements 1 and 2: strategic vision and acquisition logic, and the program opportunities the group chose.

Element Core answer
Ambition Become a science-and-technology group with AI at the centre: Lenovo as the engine, a 300+ company AI portfolio as the pipeline, and new controlled operating pillars beyond Lenovo and Levima

Logic statement: Legend should acquire only where control of a proven AI business creates value its minority stakes cannot, using Lenovo's distribution and the group's 300-company portfolio as its deal pipeline.

1 · Strategic vision and acquisition logic

Who Legend Holdings is today

  • Model: a two-wheel holding company, "industrial operations" plus "industrial incubation and investment". Operating subsidiaries: Lenovo Group (smart devices and infrastructure), Levima Group (new materials), Banque Internationale à Luxembourg (BIL, banking) and Joyvio Group (agriculture and food).
  • Scale: 2025 revenue RMB 605.9bn (+18%), net profit RMB 9.8bn (+28%), R&D above RMB 17bn, nearly 150 Chinese tech companies invested in during the year. H1 2026 revenue RMB 362.9bn (+29%), attributable net profit RMB 2.23bn (+219%), cash RMB 79.5bn.
  • Strategy: repositioned as a science-and-technology group. In H1 2026 it added more than 80 investments in AI, optical interconnect, quantum, embodied intelligence, new materials, smart healthcare, commercial aerospace and the low-altitude economy.
  • AI footprint: more than 300 AI companies invested across the Legend family, including Zhipu, Moore Threads, MetaX, Pony.ai and Cambricon. 70+ portfolio companies work commercially with Lenovo.
  • Lenovo is the engine: Legend raised its stake from 31.41% to 32.34% in late 2025. In H1 2026 Lenovo's infrastructure revenue grew 62% with a US$54bn AI server backlog, and AI-related revenue rose 64% to 36% of the total.
  • Portfolio pruning: Legend is reported (Financial Times, Sep 2026) to be working with Goldman Sachs on selling its 90% BIL stake at a valuation of about €2.5bn or more.

2 · Acquisition program opportunities: the three programs chosen

A. Technology access: a minority-to-majority ladder

  • Logic: use the information advantage of 300+ AI stakes. Over five years, raise Legend's holding to control (51%+) in 2–4 portfolio companies that have shown product-market fit, focusing on embodied intelligence and AI hardware (edge AI chips, optical interconnect). Buy around the late-stage or pre-IPO round, with Legend's funds as co-sellers.
  • Why it fits: lowest search cost and best due diligence, because Legend already sits on these boards. Its record (15 portfolio listings in 2025, 12 in H1 2026) gives a clear exit-or-control decision per holding.
  • Risks and mitigation:
    • Valuation of unproven technology → stage the ladder (e.g. 20% → 35% → 51%) on milestones, earn-outs.
    • Conflicts between the listed parent and its funds' LPs → independent valuation, independent-director approval of related-party deals.
    • Founder and key-engineer flight after control → keep founder equity and autonomy, retention packages.
    • Export controls on AI chips → screen technology exposure before each step.

B. Platform plus bolt-ons: an industrial AI solutions group

  • Logic: buy control of one mid-sized Chinese industrial-AI or "AI+" solutions company as the platform, then add 4–6 vertical AI application firms (manufacturing, energy, healthcare, logistics), many already in the portfolio. Lenovo supplies hardware and channels; Levima and Joyvio are the first reference customers.
  • Why it fits: serves China's "AI+" push and the group's AI+ application ecosystem; creates a third pillar with recurring software and service revenue.
  • Risks and mitigation:
    • A full price for the platform → choose a platform from within the portfolio where Legend has insight and a relationship.
    • Too many small integrations of people-dependent software teams → light-touch integration (shared sales, cloud and back office; keep product teams), a cap of about two bolt-ons a year.
    • Channel conflict with Lenovo's solutions and services business → define verticals and a reseller agreement up front.

C. Vertical integration: critical AI compute supply chain

  • Logic: take controlling stakes in 2–3 suppliers of components that are scarce or exposed to export controls and that Lenovo's AI servers depend on, such as optical modules and interconnect, liquid cooling and power systems, or domestic accelerator boards. Lenovo's US$54bn AI server backlog provides the anchor demand; Legend owns the assets at holding level and sells to Lenovo at arm's length.
  • Why it fits: supply security is now strategic under export controls; it captures margin along the AI hardware value chain; it uses the group's manufacturing experience (Levima) and portfolio links in AI chips.
  • Risks and mitigation:
    • Capital intensity and hardware cyclicality → minority partners, staged capex, focus on asset-light design-and-assembly assets.
    • Lost flexibility if technology shifts (e.g. copper to optical, new cooling) → keep alternative suppliers; review the program annually.
    • Related-party dependence on Lenovo and conflict with Lenovo's own procurement → arm's-length contracts, independent approval, a target that at least 40% of revenue comes from customers other than Lenovo.
    • Sanctions and export-control exposure → prefer domestic-supply segments; legal screening before each deal.

The technology-access ladder is the entry route for both other programs: the first controlled stakes become the platform for B or the first supply-chain asset for C. Budget: the group set USD 2bn of dry powder (about RMB 14bn) for the M&A team over the program. The split above leaves USD 0.2bn (10%) as a reserve for integration costs and opportunistic deals. At this size, B buys a mid-sized platform (roughly USD 300–500m) plus small bolt-ons, and C favours minority-plus-control stakes in asset-light suppliers rather than capital-heavy plants. The split and ratings are the group's working estimates, not sourced figures.

Program type set aside

  • Geographic expansion (AI-enabled industrial tech in Europe and Southeast Asia): rejected because of high regulatory risk for a Chinese AI buyer (EU foreign-investment screening, US restrictions) and because the BIL exit signals a retreat from European assets.
  • Consolidation: no fragmented core market to roll up.
